Keynote Speaker

Prof. Dr. Martin V. Butz

University of Tübingen

Contextualizing Predictive Minds and Machines

Nearly 50 years ago, Allen Newell proposed a largely overlooked Problem Space Hypothesis, which suggests that the problem space is the fundamental organizational unit of all human goal-oriented symbolic activity. In recent years, it has been suggested that humans construct their own contexts or contextual frames to be able to focus on the task at hand. I will provide some intuition for these propositions. Then, I will introduce a novel active inference architecture that shows the relevance of a suitable problem space, that is, a contextual frame, for modeling human behavior. This model derives structural learning, structural selection, structural parametrization, and planning as inference processes. The objective is always the same: to minimize anticipated surprise. Considering challenging reasoning tasks, such as the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC) Challenge, I will finally propose how human problem solving may emerge and may be designed (by evolution) to reach goals in diverse but locally stable environmental contexts, maximally flexibly and effortlessly.
